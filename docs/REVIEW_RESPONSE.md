# Response to the major-revision critique

This document maps the supplied critique to changes in the accompanying manuscript and repository. It is a research-status record, not a claim of journal acceptance.

| Reviewer concern | Revision and evidence | Remaining boundary |
|---|---|---|
| The inverse-duration noise split was chosen rather than fitted. | The abstract, methods, and discussion identify the saved calibration as one nominal point and the drive share `alpha` as an assumption. A held-out fit to published measured single-qubit duration sweeps tests the law and reveals substantial uncertainty-normalized residuals. An exploratory inverse-square variant improves leave-one-duration-out mean absolute error while retaining convexity. A finite-library decision audit now compares both laws with an empirical interpolator on identical withheld duration choices and separately estimates a training-only model-discrepancy diagnostic. A new finite-scenario minimax result and solver preserve GP structure for a declared coefficient uncertainty set. | The inverse-power laws still make identical measured choices. The empirical interpolator was examined after prior results, is not a GP law, and still has residuals larger than the source error bars. The robust scenarios have no target-device coverage interpretation until justified by calibration. External pulse-family coefficients cannot calibrate IBM CZ gates. |
| Certificates were worded as hardware guarantees. | Proof statements now name their exact model and define the purification-fidelity quantity. Dense and Monte Carlo checks are described as implementation consistency checks. | No hardware fidelity was measured. A separate simulator now probes idle decay and anisotropy, but they remain outside the proven observable model; crosstalk and leakage also remain unmodeled. |
| The original 64/128-qubit rows only screened infeasibility. | `benchmarks/run_feasible_scale.py` and `evidence/feasible_scale/` add 24 actual feasible GP and direct-convex solves at 32, 64, and 128 qubits. The per-instance target rule, solver timing, raw values, table, and figure are supplied. | These model-attainable targets are selected using the same assumed model, so the study tests computational scale, not physical validity. Single-run timings do not support a formal scaling law. |
| Comparisons were unmatched or based on an adapted heuristic. | The manuscript identifies the Lussi et al. implementation as an adaptation, makes the matched-deadline result primary, and retains small gains and regressions. The large-circuit study uses an optimal uniform policy with the same target. A new direct local comparison places GP $p=1$ and $p=2$ vectors ASAP/ALAP and evaluates them against Qiskit ASAP/ALAP with and without two-X DD on the same circuit and 168-ns deadline. It reports modest Markovian gains and a strong regression versus DD under shot-static detuning. | GP uses hypothetical continuous-duration gates, whereas Qiskit schedules fixed nominal gates: the action sets are not matched to a measured pulse library. All fidelity results are synthetic; pulse-level control and hardware-measured fidelity remain untested. |
| Uniqueness and channel-fidelity proofs were abbreviated. | Theorem 1 now states a coordinatewise positive reciprocal-coefficient condition and distinguishes duration from finish-time uniqueness. The path-flow proof defines the dual domain and derives its gap identity. The unitary-jump theorem explicitly derives the no-jump channel decomposition with an untouched reference; the general bound gives the Duhamel and no-jump arguments. | These proofs should still receive independent mathematical peer review. |
| Wording implied timeouts, exact hardware quantization, and physical breakthroughs. | The abstract, result discussion, and conclusion remove the untested timeout speedup, hardware execution implication, and broad breakthrough claims. Geometric-grid rounding is conditional on a feasible strengthened solve and numerical residual checks. | An actual pulse library and out-of-sample physical data are required before deployment claims. |

The core novelty is the particular coupling of fixed-DAG duration allocation, a path-flow bracket, and Pauli-observable constraints under an explicit channel model. Convex project crashing, geometric programming, and Pauli propagation themselves are prior art. The revised paper is stronger as a transparent methods contribution. A subjective “10/10” or Q1 acceptance claim cannot be established from local simulation alone.
